# Lesson Brief: Constant-Speed Motion

**Status:** Ready for production-pipeline candidate generation once the
`constant_speed_motion` recipe is enabled for the run (subject plan P2,
step 3: the physics pilot).

**Working CoursePack ID:** `constant-speed-motion`

**Locale:** `zh-CN`

**Subject:** physics (the first non-mathematics CoursePack)

## Natural learner request

> 老师，请用图像给我讲一节课：物体做匀速直线运动时，路程是怎样随时间变化的？我希望能拖动时间、改变速度，看到物体怎样前进、路程—时间图像怎样变化，最后学会从图像读出速度。

This request is intentionally written as a normal learner request. It does not
name OLL components, camera behavior, slider controls, layout, or internal
visual capabilities.

## Intended learner

- approximately ages 12–14;
- knows that speed tells how fast something moves, and its unit m/s;
- can read a point on a graph with labelled axes;
- may think a steeper distance–time line means a longer journey rather than a
  higher speed, or expect the line to start somewhere other than the origin.

## Required learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, the learner should be able to:

1. State that at constant speed equal times give equal distances, so distance
   is proportional to time: `s = v t`.
2. Relate the moving object's position to the point on the distance–time
   graph at the same moment.
3. Explain that the distance–time graph of constant-speed motion is a straight
   line through the origin, and that a steeper line means a higher speed.
4. Read a speed from the graph as distance divided by time.

## Candidate evaluation rubric

### Course structure

- The lesson establishes "same time, same distance" before stating `s = v t`.
- Time and speed are explored separately before they are combined.
- Every narrated beat produces a visible teaching change.

### Physics correctness

- Distance, time and speed carry consistent units (m, s, m/s).
- Every number in the narration agrees with the board (the readout, the graph
  point and the slider values); the narration invents no figures the request
  did not supply.
- The graph passes through the origin; a steeper line is described as a higher
  speed, not a longer distance.
- The lesson does not describe acceleration or variable speed as constant-speed
  motion.

### Visual grounding

- The moving object and the distance–time graph are on screen together and
  change together.
- Axes carry their quantities and units.
- Notes and formulas stay near the live visual without overlap.

### Interaction

- The learner changes both time and speed during the lesson.
- At least one task has a declared target (a time, a distance or a speed) that
  the board can judge.

### Runtime and packaging

- The OLL validates against the current production contract.
- The lesson needs no plugin feature beyond the core player: the recipe uses
  shared geometry and plot features only.
